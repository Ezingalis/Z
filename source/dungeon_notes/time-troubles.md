## Premise (transcribed/paraphrased)
The time wizard Francois De Javous (Deja vu) has called the adventurers to his pocket dimension to help him locate some very important objects he has lost. Convey that time magic has taken its toll on his mind and he is quite forgetful: he often forgets not only where he is but when he is within time and even his own timeline. He says he has lost all his keys but not the key ring. The keys are important because without them Francois cannot perform the rituals that preserve time. He has forgotten when he last had the keys, but gives a map of his home, as he often forgets what building is what. Three main locations on the map: the house, the lab, and the forest. Other locations: a pond, a library, a small garden, a sheep pen, an outhouse, and a once neat lawn. There are 4 keys. Francois gifts each adventurer a key detector that beeps when within 20 feet of a key and gets louder and faster as they get closer.
Time loop: the pocket dimension is in a constant loop between 6:00 p.m. and 7:00 p.m.; when it hits 7:00, time loops to 6:00 and all keys return to their original locations.

## Locations (each holds one key)
- The House: white stone mansion, two floors. A key is in a rodent's nest somewhere in the house along with other treasures. The mouse knows the key is valuable and is defensive of it at first, but is willing to sell it to the highest bidder (players, fairies, or brownies).
- The Lab: messy building with bubbling potions and bottles connected by tubes along side clockwork windup inventions. A key is hidden within an automaton. When characters attempt to retrieve it, the automaton wakes up and questions them about why they are trying to reach inside its clockwork body. If they fight it, the other automatons wake up and join the fight.
- The Forest: forever in the shadows of towering redwoods and pines. Another key is somewhere along the path leading to a clearing where the wizard sometimes performs rituals. The key hangs from a branch of a tree or bush, partially concealed by leaves and branches. Just as characters are about to grasp it, it is snatched by a fairy and hidden deeper in the forest; this happens three times until they can finally retrieve it from 40 to 50 feet up a tree.
- The Outhouse/Lawn: lawn covered with leaves, branches and trinkets Francois probably dropped. A key here is protected by "playful" fairies that stole all the keys and hid them. The fairies are actively trying to protect the key from the brownies and are in combat with them over it. The key is constantly changing hands and moving around; emphasize by having it change locations every turn. After 3-5 rounds it ends up in the outhouse in the toilet unless the characters retrieve it before. Both factions are willing to talk about giving up the key, but if you tell the fairies you intend to return them, they will attack and ignore anything you say. If you tell the brownies you want to keep the keys or hide them, they will ignore you and attack. Can be flavored as combat or a skill challenge; emphasize they are viciously fighting for it.
