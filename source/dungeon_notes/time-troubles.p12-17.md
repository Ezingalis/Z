## Premise / hook
The time wizard Francois De Javous (Deja vu) has called the adventurers to his pocket dimension to help him find some very important objects he has lost. The GM is told to convey that time magic has taken its toll on his mind and he is quite forgetful. He often forgets not only where he is but when he is within time and even his own timeline. He says he seems to have lost all of his keys but not the key ring. The keys are important because without them Francois cannot perform the rituals that preserve time. He has forgotten when he last had the keys.

Francois gives the party:
- a map of his home (he often forgets what building is what);
- information that there are three main locations of interest on the map: the house, the lab, and the forest;
- other locations on the map: a pond, a library, a small garden, a sheep pen, an outhouse, and a once neat lawn;
- the fact that there are 4 keys;
- a key detector for each adventurer, which beeps when within 20 feet of a key and gets louder and faster as they get closer.

Time loop: Francois's pocket dimension is in a constant time loop between 6:00 p.m. and 7:00 p.m. When it hits 7:00, time loops to 6:00 and all keys return to their original locations.

Objective: recover the 4 keys (one per location below: house, lab, forest, outhouse/lawn) before the hour loops.

## Locations

### The House
- White stone mansion with two floors.
- A key is in a rodent's nest somewhere in the house, along with other treasures (not itemized).
- The mouse knows the key is valuable and is defensive of it at first, but is willing to sell it to the highest bidder (bidders can be the players, fairies, or brownies).

### The Lab
- A messy building with bubbling potions and bottles connected by tubes alongside clockwork windup inventions.
- A key is hidden within an automaton.
- When the characters attempt to retrieve it, the automaton wakes up and questions them about why they are trying to reach inside its clockwork body.
- If the characters fight it, the other automatons wake up and join the fight.

### The Forest
- Forever in the shadows of towering redwoods and pines.
- Another key is somewhere along the path leading to a clearing where the wizard sometimes performs rituals.
- The key hangs from a branch of a tree or bush, partially concealed by leaves and branches.
- Just as the characters are about to grasp it, it is snatched by a fairy and hidden deeper in the forest. This happens three times until they can finally retrieve it from 40 to 50 feet up a tree.

### The Outhouse/Lawn
- The lawn is covered with leaves, branches, and trinkets that Francois probably dropped and forgot about.
- A key here is protected by "playful" fairies that stole all of the keys and hid them. The fairies are actively trying to protect the key from the brownies and are in combat with them over it.
- The key is constantly changing hands and moving around. GM should emphasize this by having it change locations every turn. After 3-5 rounds, it ends up in the outhouse in the toilet unless the characters retrieve it before it ends up there.
- Both factions are willing to talk about giving up the key. But if you tell the fairies that you intend to return the keys, they will attack and ignore anything you have to say. If you tell the brownies that you want to keep the keys or hide them, they will ignore you and attack.
- The section can be flavored as combat or as a skill challenge to obtain the key; GM should just emphasize that they are viciously fighting for it.

## The Factions
- The Brownies are trying to help recover the keys for Francois.
- The Fairies are hiding them.
