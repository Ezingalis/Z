# Milosh and the Rotting Well: Learn-to-Play Audio Script

Oct 20, 2026 · @Ethan Zingalis

## How to use this script

This is a 22-chapter, learn-to-play audio drama: you listen to a table of four players and a DM, and you learn D&D by hearing every roll, modifier, and decision said out loud. Chapter 1 is written in full below; the plan lays out the other 21.

**Speaker tags.** Every line starts with a speaker in caps, ready for ElevenLabs multi-speaker rendering. `[OOC]` means a player is speaking as themselves (asking a rule, joking). No tag means they're speaking in character. Emotion cues like `[laughs]` or `[whispering]` are for the voice model.

**Sound cues.** `[SFX: ...]` is a sound effect and `[MUSIC: ...]` is a music bed. Dice rolls always get `[SFX: dice roll]` so you hear the moment of truth.

**How the teaching works.** Early chapters say every roll as math: "d20 shows 16, plus my +5, that's 21." The DM explains a rule the first time it comes up, once, in a sentence or two. By Chapter 7 the table stops spelling out the basics, the same way a real group would.

**Milosh starts at level 1.** He's the same character as your real campaign (Charlatan, Wild Magic sorcerer, comedic "failed wizard"), rebuilt from level 1 so you hear each level's new abilities arrive. Wild Magic itself unlocks at level 3, in Chapter 6. His business cards do not appear at all until the finale arc.

**Runtime.** Chapter 1 runs about 7,000 words, roughly 50 minutes of speech. With music beds, sound effects, and dice pauses it lands near 60 minutes.

## Campaign plan: Full Circle

The party frees Findlay's Lamp Emporium from a devil named Belvedere, fails to stop him escaping with his horn, chases him through six other dungeons from your PDF, and comes back to the Emporium for the finale: full circle, the contest's own theme.

**The through-line.** In Chapter 5 Belvedere gets his second horn, doubles in size and strength, and escapes through the Hell Realm portal. Every later arc either delays him or hands the party a tool they'll need to beat him. Findlay is the recurring patron: a real wizard with his own shop, which is everything Milosh pretends to be.

**The business cards.** Absent until Chapter 20, when Milosh reaches level 9 and learns *Animate Objects*. The finale has him animate every card he's ever printed. If you had a different spell in mind for that payoff, it swaps in cleanly.

## Chapter 1: Under New Management

The party takes Findlay's job, talks their way into the Showroom, and fights their first imps; Milosh learns what a d20 is for. Rules taught: ability scores and modifiers, skill checks, proficiency, DCs, passive Perception, group checks, advantage, initiative, the turn, attack rolls vs. AC, damage, and immunity.

### Scene 1: The man with nine fingers

&#91;MUSIC: warm tavern ambience, low fire crackle, fades under\]

DM: \[OOC\] Alright, everybody settle in. Milosh, this is your first game, so here's the deal. I describe the world. You tell me what your character does. When the outcome is uncertain, you roll a twenty-sided die, add a number from your sheet, and I tell you what happens. That's ninety percent of D&D.

MILOSH: \[OOC\] And the other ten percent?

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] Arguing about whether you can lick the mysterious potion.

OSKAR: \[OOC\] \[laughs\] You can always lick the potion.

DM: \[OOC\] We'll get there. Let's start.

DM: The city of Grellmoor, a rainy Tuesday. The Bent Kettle tavern smells like wet wool and onion soup. Four of you share a corner table, mostly because nobody else would sit near the man in the bow tie who keeps waving his bandaged hand. Quick introductions. Tamsin?

TAMSIN: Tamsin Reedwhistle. Halfling. Professional retriever of things people didn't want retrieved. \[OOC\] I'm a rogue. Sneaky, stabby, good with locks.

OSKAR: Oskar Dahl! Formerly of the City Watch, currently of this chair. \[OOC\] Fighter. Big armor, big sword. I stand in front of you all and get hit.

IMELDA: Sister Imelda of the Lantern Order. I keep people alive, usually against their wishes. \[OOC\] Cleric. Healing and holy fire.

DM: And Milosh?

MILOSH: \[clears throat, grandly\] Milosh. Sorcerer. Entrepreneur. You may have heard I left the Academy of the Arcane Arts. That's true. I left to pursue business opportunities.

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] Were you expelled?

MILOSH: \[OOC\] He was very expelled.

&#91;SFX: tavern door bangs open, rain, bell jingles\]

DM: \[as FINDLAY, frantic, high\] Adventurers! Oh thank the stars, actual adventurers! \[normal\] A thin man with wild orange hair rushes over. Blue bow tie. Brown jacket soaked through. His left hand is wrapped in a bandage, and you notice the bandage is shaped like four fingers, not five.

DM: \[as FINDLAY\] I'm Findlay! Owner of Findlay's Lamp Emporium on Artisan Avenue! Yesterday I spilled tea on my demonic contracts. The tea dissolved the ink. The ink was the only thing holding my imp workforce in line! And my devil servant, Belvedere! They've taken my whole shop!

OSKAR: Imps make your lamps?

DM: \[as FINDLAY\] Who did you think made lamps? One of them bit off my finger! They've put up a magic ward so I can't get back in, and Belvedere said if I try anything, he'll burn my spellbook!

IMELDA: That's horrible. What do you need?

DM: \[as FINDLAY\] Sneak in. Quietly! Belvedere has a second horn hidden in a secret compartment in the commode in my bedroom. If he finds it, he gets his full power back, and then nobody's getting in. \[normal\] He slaps a brass key and a list on the table. Tamsin, you grab the list. Read it out.

TAMSIN: "Four gold per imp tail returned. Twenty-five gold, retrieve my severed finger. Forty, send Belvedere back through the portal. Eighty, destroy the ward. One hundred, retrieve my spellbook. One hundred twenty, retrieve the blueprints for making lamps."

OSKAR: \[OOC\] That's a lot of gold.

DM: \[OOC\] It is, at level 1. A night at an inn is a few silver. You'd be rich.

IMELDA: \[OOC\] I'm not collecting imp tails.

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] We'll discuss the tails.

DM: \[as FINDLAY, peering at Milosh\] And you! Academy robes! A fellow wizard! You'll know a ward when you see one. You can take it apart, can't you?

MILOSH: \[OOC\] Oh no. Okay. I want to lie. I want him to think I'm a real wizard.

DM: \[OOC\] Perfect, that's your first roll. When you try to convince someone of something that isn't true, that's a Deception check. Look at your character sheet. Find the word Deception. What's the number next to it?

MILOSH: \[OOC\] Plus five.

DM: \[OOC\] Where does the five come from? Two places. First, your Charisma score is 16, which gives a modifier of +3. Every ability score turns into a modifier: 10 is zero, every two points above that is another +1. Second, you're proficient in Deception from your Charlatan background. Being proficient means you add your proficiency bonus, which is +2 at level 1. Three plus two, five. Roll a d20 and add five.

MILOSH: \[OOC\] Here goes.

&#91;SFX: dice roll\]

MILOSH: \[OOC\] Fourteen! Plus five, nineteen.

DM: \[OOC\] I set a number you need to hit, called the DC, for difficulty class. Findlay is desperate and wants to believe you, so I set it at 12. Nineteen clears it easily. Go ahead and say it.

MILOSH: \[confident\] Wards? Findlay, I've dismantled wards before breakfast. At the Academy they called me "the Unwarder." Well, they called me other things too. But mostly that.

DM: \[as FINDLAY, overjoyed\] The Unwarder! I knew it! \[normal\] He pumps your hand with his good hand. Then he leans in and whispers: the ward is on the top floor, in a circle on a pedestal. His severed finger is holding it in place.

IMELDA: \[OOC\] So destroying the ward and getting the finger might be the same job.

DM: \[OOC\] Good catch. That's the game, too: noticing how pieces fit.

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] Milosh, you know you just promised to undo a ward.

MILOSH: \[OOC\] I know.

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] Do you know how to undo a ward?

MILOSH: \[OOC\] I have a plus two in Arcana.

OSKAR: \[OOC\] \[laughs\] We're going to be fine.

## Review: three passes on Chapter 1

Chapter 1 was reviewed three times, once each for storytelling, D&D rules, and teaching; the fixes marked "Fixed" are already in the script above. These were review passes I ran in three roles, not three separate people, so a read by a real DM friend is still worth it before recording.

| Reviewer | Finding | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rules | Imelda wears chain mail (AC 18), which is heavy armor, so she should have had disadvantage on the group Stealth check too | Fixed: she now rolls two dice and takes the lower |
| Rules | Knocking a creature out in the 2024 rules leaves it at 1 hit point and unconscious, not at zero; that line was also missing its \[OOC\] tag | Fixed |
| Rules | Checked and correct: Fire Bolt 1d10, Healing Word 2d4 plus modifier, Sacred Flame 1d8 radiant on a Dex save, prone and smoke-cloud disadvantage, Sneak Attack trigger, Milosh's +2 Dex save | No change |
| Teaching | "You only get two spell slots per day" came up before spell slots were ever explained | Fixed: the DM explains slots and long rests right after Healing Word |
| Teaching | Saving throws arrive mid-fight, which is a lot at once; Chapter 2 is built around saves to reinforce them | Kept, and covered again in Chapter 2 |
| Teaching | Perception vs. Investigation and Persuasion vs. Deception are each explained once, in the moment, in two sentences | No change: this is the right density |
| Story | Milosh's closing line said he lied to four people, but he lied to five | Fixed: "I lied to everyone I met" |
| Story | His "Unwarder" boast in Scene 1 needs a payoff | Fixed in the plan: Chapter 5 makes him face the ward |
| Story | The failed group Stealth check pays off as a comedy beat and gives Fog a reason to flee, which sets up Belvedere releasing Pepe | No change: failure moves the story forward |
| Story | Bonbons, Max's revolution, and the unidentified potion are all planted for Chapters 2 to 4 | No change |

### Scene 2: Closed for business

&#91;MUSIC: light rain, distant city bustle\]

DM: Twenty minutes later you stand on Artisan Avenue in the drizzle. Findlay's Lamp Emporium is a narrow stone tower, four floors tall, round like a stack of coins. A chimney on the second floor puffs out smoke that smells, oddly, like mint. The front door has a hand-painted sign nailed to it.

OSKAR: I read the sign.

DM: "Closed for business. Opening soon under new management. Signed, B."

IMELDA: B for Belvedere.

DM: \[OOC\] Before you go in, I'm going to check something without asking you to roll. Everyone has a passive Perception: it's 10 plus your Perception modifier. It's how alert you are when you're not actively searching, and I use it so I don't tip you off by saying "roll Perception." Tamsin, yours is the highest, 14. So you notice something.

TAMSIN: Ooh.

DM: Through the front window, past the dusty display lamps, you see movement. Small shapes. Three of them, on ladders, painting something on the inside wall.

TAMSIN: I want to get a better look. Can I sneak up to the window?

DM: \[OOC\] Yes. That's Stealth, which runs on Dexterity. Tamsin, you have Expertise in Stealth. Expertise means you add your proficiency bonus twice. So your Dexterity +3, plus 2, plus 2 again. That's why your sheet says +7.

MILOSH: \[OOC\] Wait, she gets to double it?

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] Rogues are good at exactly a few things. This is one of them.

&#91;SFX: dice roll\]

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] Thirteen. Plus seven, twenty.

DM: You slip along the wall like a puddle that learned to walk. Nobody inside looks up. Through the glass, you see three imps. Imps are little devils, about knee-high on Oskar, with leathery wings and tails. One is green and broad, built like a tiny bouncer, holding a paint bucket. One is gray and black with a striped tail like a skunk. The third is dark gray and wheezes, and every few seconds a puff of black smoke leaks from his mouth. They're painting a sign above the counter. So far it reads: "BELVEDERE'S LAMP EMPOR."

TAMSIN: \[whispering\] Three imps. Painting a sign. The smoky one looks like a snitch.

OSKAR: \[OOC\] Can we just go in and fight them?

IMELDA: \[OOC\] Findlay said be quiet. If we start a brawl on the first floor, Belvedere hears it upstairs.

MILOSH: I have a plan.

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] That's what I was afraid of.

MILOSH: \[OOC\] Belvedere is the new management, right? So I walk in like I'm from corporate. Head office. I'm here to inspect the new signage.

DM: \[OOC\] I love that. Here's how I'd run it. Milosh walks in the front door with confidence. The rest of you sneak around to the side door Tamsin spotted, so you're in position if it goes wrong. That's a group Stealth check for the three of you.

IMELDA: \[OOC\] What's a group check?

DM: \[OOC\] When several people do the same thing together, everyone rolls. If at least half the group succeeds, the whole group succeeds. The good sneaker covers for the clanky one. The DC is 12.

OSKAR: \[OOC\] I'm the clanky one. Chain mail gives me disadvantage on Stealth.

DM: \[OOC\] Right. Disadvantage means you roll two d20s and take the lower one. Advantage is the opposite: roll two, take the higher. Lots of things in the game give one or the other, and that's all they ever mean.

&#91;SFX: dice roll, two dice\]

OSKAR: \[OOC\] A seventeen and a four. I take the four. Plus zero. Four.

&#91;SFX: dice roll\]

IMELDA: \[OOC\] Same problem. My chain mail is heavy armor too, so disadvantage. Eleven and a six. I take the six, plus zero. Six.

DM: \[OOC\] Also short. Tamsin?

&#91;SFX: dice roll\]

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] Nine, plus seven, sixteen.

DM: \[OOC\] One success, two failures. Not half. The group fails, but failing doesn't always mean disaster. Oskar, as you step around the corner, your armor goes clank, clank, clank. Imelda, you bump a rain barrel.

&#91;SFX: wooden barrel tipping, water splashing\]

DM: Inside, the smoky imp's head snaps up toward the side of the building. He wheezes, "Did you hear that?"

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] Milosh. You need to go in. Right now.

MILOSH: \[OOC\] I'm going in. I'm a professional.

&#91;SFX: shop door opens, little brass bell jingles\]

### Scene 3: Corporate has entered the building

&#91;MUSIC: plucky, sneaky pizzicato strings\]

DM: The Showroom is round, cluttered, and dim. Lamps everywhere: brass lamps, glass lamps, one shaped like a duck. Pink velvet couches. A counter with a cash register. A spiral staircase in the middle winds up into the ceiling. And three imps on ladders freeze mid-brushstroke and stare at you.

DM: \[OOC\] When you meet a creature and I don't already know how it feels about you, I roll to see its mood. This adventure has a little table for imps: friendly, curious, shy, afraid, angry, murderous.

&#91;SFX: three dice rolling behind a screen\]

DM: \[OOC\] The green one, Bif, is curious. The skunk-tailed one, Poo, is friendly. The smoky one, Fog, is angry. So you have a fan, a maybe, and a problem.

MILOSH: \[OOC\] Okay. Corporate voice. \[in character, booming\] Good afternoon! Regional Office. I'm here about the sign.

DM: \[as BIF, slow and deep\] ...The sign?

MILOSH: The sign. Belvedere's Lamp Empor. Empor. Do you know what an "empor" is, gentlemen? Neither do our customers. Head office is very concerned.

DM: \[OOC\] That's Deception again, and this one matters more. Three imps, one already angry. DC 15.

MILOSH: \[OOC\] So I roll the d20, add my Charisma modifier, which is +3, plus proficiency, which is +2, because I'm proficient in Deception. Plus five.

DM: \[OOC\] Exactly. You've got it.

&#91;SFX: dice roll\]

MILOSH: \[OOC\] Sixteen! Sixteen plus five is twenty-one!

DM: \[OOC\] Twenty-one beats 15. But Fog gets a chance to see through you. When someone lies to a creature that's suspicious, the creature can make a Wisdom check using Insight to read the liar. It's a contest: whoever rolls higher wins. Fog needs to beat your 21.

&#91;SFX: dice roll behind screen\]

DM: \[OOC\] Fog rolls a 9, plus 1. Ten. Not even close.

DM: \[as BIF, impressed\] Head office.

DM: \[as POO, giggly\] Ooh, I knew it looked wrong! I said "empor" looked wrong!

DM: \[as FOG, wheezing\] Nobody told us about any head office.

MILOSH: That's what makes it a surprise inspection.

DM: \[normal\] Fog squints. He coughs out a little puff of smoke. And then from the side of the building comes, once again, clank. Clank. Clank.

DM: \[as FOG\] What is THAT?

MILOSH: That's... my security detail. Very standard. Oskar! Come in! Show them your... \[OOC\] What does a security detail have?

OSKAR: \[OOC\] I kick the side door open and walk in with my sword out.

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] Oskar, no.

OSKAR: \[OOC\] It's what security does!

&#91;SFX: door kicked open, wood splinters\]

DM: Oskar fills the doorway, sword drawn, rain dripping off his chain mail. Sister Imelda is behind him, wet to the knees from the barrel. Tamsin is technically also there, but she's already crouched behind a pink couch, and nobody's noticed her.

DM: \[as FOG, shrieking\] Liars! Intruders! I'm telling Belvedere!

DM: \[OOC\] Fog's flying for the stairs. That means combat. Everybody, roll initiative.

&#91;MUSIC: combat sting, then driving drums under\]

MILOSH: \[OOC\] What's initiative?

DM: \[OOC\] It decides the order of turns. Roll a d20 and add your Dexterity modifier. Highest goes first, and we go around in that order every round. A round is about six seconds in the story.

&#91;SFX: four dice roll\]

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] Sixteen plus three, nineteen.

MILOSH: \[OOC\] Twelve plus two, fourteen.

OSKAR: \[OOC\] Nine.

IMELDA: \[OOC\] Six. I'm a dwarf in armor, I'm not fast.

DM: \[OOC\] The order is Tamsin at 19, Fog at 15, Milosh at 14, Bif at 11, Oskar at 9, Poo at 8, Imelda at 6.

DM: \[OOC\] One more thing before we start, Milosh. On your turn you get three things: you can move up to your speed, which is 30 feet for all of you. You get one action, like attacking or casting a spell. And sometimes you get a bonus action, but only if something on your sheet says it uses one. That's it. Move, action, maybe a bonus action.

MILOSH: \[OOC\] Move, action, maybe bonus. Got it.

#### Round 1

DM: Tamsin, you're up. Fog is flapping toward the spiral staircase, about 20 feet from you.

TAMSIN: I throw a dagger at him. \[OOC\] Attack roll: d20 plus 5. That's my Dexterity +3 and my proficiency +2.

&#91;SFX: dice roll\]

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] Twelve, plus five, seventeen.

DM: \[OOC\] Every creature has an Armor Class, AC, which is the number you need to meet or beat to hit it. Fog's AC is 12. Seventeen hits. Roll damage.

TAMSIN: \[OOC\] Dagger is a d4 plus my Dexterity. \[SFX: small die\] Two, plus three, five. No Sneak Attack, though, right?

DM: \[OOC\] Right. Quick explanation for Milosh: Sneak Attack is Tamsin's big extra damage, but she only gets it if she has advantage on the attack, or if one of her allies is right next to the target. Nobody's next to Fog. So, five damage.

DM: The dagger thunks into Fog's wing. He yelps and wobbles in the air, but he keeps going. Fog's turn. He gulps, and belches a huge black cloud of smoke right on top of himself, and flaps higher up the stairs inside it.

&#91;SFX: deep wet belch, then a hiss of smoke\]

DM: Milosh, you're up. Fog is a shape in a cloud of smoke, about 30 feet away and climbing.

MILOSH: I blast him. Fire Bolt.

IMELDA: \[OOC\] Milosh, wait.

MILOSH: FIRE BOLT.

DM: \[OOC\] Okay! Fire Bolt is a cantrip, a spell you can cast as often as you want without using a spell slot. It's a spell attack, so you roll d20 plus your spell attack bonus, which is +5. But he's inside a cloud you can't see through, so you have disadvantage. Two d20s, take the lower.

&#91;SFX: two dice roll\]

MILOSH: \[OOC\] Eighteen and fifteen. So fifteen. Plus five, twenty!

DM: \[OOC\] That hits. Roll damage, a d10.

&#91;SFX: dice roll\]

MILOSH: \[OOC\] Eight! Eight fire damage!

DM: A bolt of fire streaks into the smoke and hits Fog square in the chest. The flame splashes off him like water off a duck. From inside the cloud, you hear him laugh.

DM: \[OOC\] Fog is immune to fire. He takes zero.

MILOSH: \[OOC\] Zero?

IMELDA: \[OOC\] He's a devil, Milosh. They're from a place that's on fire.

DM: \[OOC\] This is worth remembering. Damage comes in types: fire, cold, slashing, radiant, and so on. Some creatures are resistant to a type and take half. Some are immune and take none. Fire and devils is the classic bad match.

MILOSH: \[OOC\] In my defense, it was a very good roll.

DM: The smoke cloud drifts up the stairwell. Fog is gone, up to the second floor. Bif's turn. He drops his paint bucket, grabs a lamp stand like a club, and charges you, Milosh.
